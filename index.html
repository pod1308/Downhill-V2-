Who it's for

People who fund, plan or argue for places.

Writing a grant

  • Whether a tract is designated, and on which criterion
  • Residents and designated share for a whole service area
  • A tract table to attach, sources in the header

Briefing a member

  • Assembly, Senate and Congressional district for any point
  • One summary for a district, county or AB 617 community
  • A brief to paste, with limits and citations attached

Working on a site

  • Every regulated site within 1 to 10 km, each linked to its EPA file
  • What was measured after similar closures elsewhere
  • Where runoff from the site travels, and roughly how fast

Serving tribal communities

  • Tribal lands from three federal boundary sources
  • Designated in their own right, not through a tract score
  • Flagged where a tract is far larger than the land inside it

Limits

What this can't tell you.

A score is a rank, not a reading.

95 means the tract sits above 95% of California tracts on that one measure. It isn't a concentration of anything.

It isn't the record of designation.

It reads CalEPA's published list. Before applying for funding, confirm on CARB's Priority Populations map.

No forecasts.

No "remove this facility" button. Ranks are relative, site measures are counts rather than emissions, and OEHHA rules out before-and-after use.

Studies are from elsewhere.

They show what was measured after real closures. They don't predict a number for a site here.

Sites and scores overlap.

Six measures are counts of facilities, so a site next to a high score is partly circular.

No property prices.

A price next to a facility invites a causal reading the data can't back. Housing burden is already one of the 23 measures.

No health claims.

CalEnviroScreen is a screening tool. OEHHA says it can't assess risk to a person or an address.
